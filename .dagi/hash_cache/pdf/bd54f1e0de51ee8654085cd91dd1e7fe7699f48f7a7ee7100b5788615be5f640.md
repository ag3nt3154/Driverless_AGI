## 1. Introduction

The attempt to accurately forecast  the  future  trajectory  of  asset prices, and thus identify the optimal timing for buying or selling a stock,  stands  as  one  of  the  most  enduring  pursuits  of  investors  [1, 2].  predicting  stock  values  remains  a  daunting  task  due  to  the  inherent unpredictability and complexity of financial markets. In this context, investors, financial analysts, and researchers can be valuable resources  for  understanding  past  and  recent  trends  in  stock  market prediction,  as  noted  in  the  surveys  [3-5].  In  general,  the  models and techniques employed to predict the future prices of financial assets are confined to quantitative finance. Among the best-known are the Auto-Regressive Conditional Heteroscedasticity ( ARCH ) model, the Generalized  Auto-Regressive  Moving  Average  ( GARCH )  model,  the Auto-Regressive Moving Average ( ARIMA ) model, and fuzzy time series (see  [4,6]  for  more  extensive  details  about  these  models).  Despite extensive research dedicated to developing effective prediction methods and leveraging various mathematical models different from those mentioned previously [7], the formal mathematics used to forecast the behavior of financial asset prices encompasses relatively new developments, some of which are still under construction and have recently been reinvented with numerous machine learning techniques.

## 2. Simple forecast with time series

The mathematical study of the behavior of financial assets will thus require the formal treatment of random experiments, which was made possible  by  the  model  developed  during  the  first  third  of  the  20th century, which begins with the definition of the probability space [12]. Regarding a stochastic model for forecasting the price of equity assets, the sample space essentially encompasses all possible prices that a stock can reach. Even though in practice the price of a stock, representing the ownership rights of a company, should not assume negative values, theoretically, there is nothing to prevent this possibility.

In  a  random  experiment  where  an  investor  wishes  to  know  the price  of  a  stock  (and  therefore  expects  a  specific  value  or  a  very tight interval), it may not be the same as when the investor wants to know if the price of a stock will rise or fall. In the latter experiment, three classes could be defined for the sample space: greater than the present value, equal to the present value, or less than the present value, unlike the first example where the elements of the sample space can be infinite. We are talking about the same scenario 𝛺 , but with a different measurable space. In terms of establishing a set where experiments of interest to the observer are found, the measurable space is defined.

Definition 1 ( [13], p 161 ) . A measurable space is a pair ( 𝛺 ,  ) , where 𝛺 stands for the set of possible outcomes of the random experiment, and  denotes the set of events in the random experiment. This set of events is in turn a 𝜎 -algebra that allows for the grouping of certain subsets of 𝛺 .

A function 𝑃 with domain in  and codomain in the interval 0 ≤ 𝑃 ( 𝑥 ) ≤ 1 ∶ 𝑥 ∈  establishes a measure on the space defined in the 𝜎 -algebra.

Definition 2 ( [13], p 23 ) . A probability space is a triplet ( 𝛺 ,  , 𝑃 ) , where 𝛺 is a Sample Space,  is a 𝜎 -algebra of subsets of 𝛺 , and 𝑃 is a probability measure established over  .

According to the definition of probability function, the sum of the probabilities of all mutually exclusive events in the random experiment is equal to 1. Therefore, we have identified a probability function for a set of events of interest that group elements from the sample space; a  stochastic  process  is  the  mechanism  by  which  realizations  of  the experiment are carried out.

Definition 3 ( [13], p 482 ) . A  stochastic  process  is  a  collection  of random variables 𝑋𝑡 ∶ 𝑡 ∈ 𝑇 , parameterized by a set 𝑇 , that, independently, can take values within the sample space 𝛺 , and collectively take values within the 𝜎 -algebra  of subsets of 𝛺 .

The set 𝑇 in the definition is the parameter space containing the indices of the stochastic process.

Definition 4 ( [13], p 482 ) . The parameter space or set of system times, 𝑇 , is a set in which the stochastic process is indexed or parameterized: 𝑋𝑡 ∶ 𝑡 ∈ 𝑇 . If 𝑇 ⊆ N and consequently 𝑋𝑛 ∶ 𝑛 = 0 , 1 , … , the stochastic process is discrete-time.

Random variables are defined as those associated with the behavior of  a  system  that  does  not  evolve  completely  deterministically,  but rather obeys some stochastic mechanism. A stochastic process can also be  represented  by  a  function  of  two  variables: 𝑋 ∶ 𝑇 × 𝛺 ↦ R , where for every 𝑡 ∈ 𝑇 , the function 𝜔 ↦ 𝑋𝑡 ( 𝜔 ) is a random variable, and  for  every 𝜔 ∈ 𝛺 ,  the  function 𝑡 ↦ 𝑋𝑡 ( 𝜔 ) is  a  trajectory  of the process. With this model, it is possible to outline the evolution of a random system over time [12]. ## 2.1. Banco Santander stock price time series

Upon initial examination of the daily closing price data for SAN (refer to Fig. 2.1), it is not immediately apparent whether the ARIMA method should be employed or how the adjustment parameters should be  interpreted  in  terms  of  the  autoregressive  and  moving  average properties of the SAN time series. Therefore, it becomes necessary to assess the stationarity of this time series, which is conducted using the  Dickey-Fuller  test.  The  test  results  indicate  that  the  alternative hypothesis of stationarity can be accepted, with a 𝑃 -value of 0.7805 and a Dickey-Fuller statistic of -1 . 5093 .

Despite the strong indication of stationarity in the SAN time series, the autocorrelation and partial autocorrelation analysis shown in Fig. 2.2 do not provide sufficient information to establish the fitting parameters of the ARIMA model. Furthermore, the analysis seems to suggest the presence of a trend component in the time series [29], which contradicts the initial indication of stationarity.

As previously mentioned, for non-stationary time series, it is advisable to use the ▵ difference operator an appropriate number of times. Experience has shown that there is typically no need for differentiations beyond ▵ 2 ; in [30] authors point out that differentiating beyond the second  order  can  introduce  excessive  noise  and  distortions,  which can reduce model accuracy rather than improve it. First-order differencing often addresses the majority of trend-related non-stationarity, while second-order differencing can capture more complex patterns without destabilizing the variance. Limiting differencing to two orders maintains  the  integrity  of  the  data's  structure,  providing  a  balance between achieving stationarity  and  preserving  the  series'  predictive power. Moreover, excessive differentiation could impact the identification of the generating process [29]. Based on Definition 6, it can be inferred that for 𝑑 ≥ 3 , there is a higher likelihood of obtaining an integrated series in practice.

## 6. Conclusions and future scope

In  this  paper,  we  present  a  novel  forecasting  model  structured around  a  probability  space,  which  consists  of  a  measurable  space derived  from  a  mathematical  filtration.  Within  this  framework,  we introduce two distinct 𝜎 -algebras: one related to the probability space generated by an ARIMA ( 𝑝, 𝑑 , 𝑞 ) model applied to the asset's price, and another associated with a probability space constructed from a random walk that is parameterized by a probability function accounting for step size and reflecting the asset's historical behavior. The model is systematically developed, with its structure rigorously formulated and its key findings condensed through Observations 1 to 4 and Proposition 1.

This model was applied over 25 assets from three of the most important stock markets in the world and the Colombian stock exchange. The general result was that on 24 of these assets, a prediction was obtained, and only for one asset no decisive result was obtained. For the 24 predictions of our model, in 15 of them, a successful result was an accomplishment, meaning an accuracy of 62.5%. On the other hand, it is interesting to note that for the United States stock market, the percentage of success in the accuracy of the model was 100%, for Spain was 80%, while for Colombia and Japan was 40%. We remark that the probability space discussed in this article has been configured for binary - and mutually exclusive - events (bullish or bearish trend), and therefore, the model aims to forecast trends rather than specific quotes. The resulting predictions can be useful in making investment strategy decisions, going short or long in a certain market strategy.

As a continuation of this research, the model we have proposed offers  a  vast  array  of  potential  variations,  which  will  undoubtedly enable  us  to  develop  other  hybrid  models  or  entirely  new  models. Specifically, we are exploring the following lines of research:

- (1) We anticipate that subsequent analyses will enable us to increase the cardinality of the 𝜎 -algebra within the probability space under investigation. By establishing appropriate quotation environments, we aim to anticipate the associated probability functions corresponding to specific price intervals. This approach will facilitate the estimation of the expected value of the asset over the forecast horizon ℎ , thereby enhancing tactical decision-making related to expected returns.
- (2) We aim to obtain different 𝜎 -algebras for the probability space by considering various classical econometric models, including Vector Autoregression ( VAR ), Error Correction Models ( ECM ), Vector Error Correction Models ( VECM ), and ( GARCH ), among others.

Finally, the application of Artificial Intelligence to generate new econometric models for analyzing time-dependent series offers complexity beyond that of classical ARIMA or GARCH models. Examples include artificial  neural  networks  [35,36]  and  recurrent  neural  networks [37,38]. However, in many cases, these new models operate as black boxes, providing no insight into their internal workings [39]. Despite this limitation, a variety of new and innovative applications are  beginning  to  emerge  on  the  research  horizon.  In  this  context, we propose the following ideas for immediate incorporation into our model:

- (3) While not excluding the potential integration of probability distribution functions derived from GARCH and other classical time series models to capture the complex, non-linear patterns inherent in stock prices, it may also be beneficial to apply filtering methods that combine the strengths of ARIMA models with conventional and deep learning techniques, such as Artificial Neural Networks ( ANN ), Support Vector Machines ( SVM ), Random Forests ( RF ) [40], Long Short-Term Memory ( LSTM ), Gated Recurrent Unit ( GRU ) networks [41-43], and wavelet neural networks ( WNNs ) [44]. This approach shows promise for enhancing the accuracy of short-term stock price trend predictions.
- (4) Our model's predictions are primarily based on historical price data, without accounting for external variables such as trading volume, macroeconomic indicators, or corporate events. By not incorporating these factors, the model may yield less accurate predictions, particularly in volatile or reactive markets. In this context, sentiment analysis, a subfield of natural language processing, has gained prominence in recent years as a valuable tool for understanding stock prices by analyzing news articles, social media posts [45], and financial reports [46]. Therefore, exploring how news sentiment, as a trend for decision support, can enhance news-based trading in our model will undoubtedly lead to a more precise forecasting approach.
- (5) As an extension of the previous point, considering that news trading combines real-time market data and natural language processing to detect relevant news announcements that trigger transactions,  these  mechanisms  are  often  part  of  algorithmic trading  systems,  such  as  Decision  Support  Systems  based  on trading volume data [47]. Surprisingly, Large Language Models like ChatGPT, known for their ability to predict the next word in a sequence, have begun to demonstrate good performance in the challenging task of predicting stock price movements using news headlines [48]. This presents numerous opportunities to incorporate these techniques into our future models.
